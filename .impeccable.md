## Design Context

### Users

People using DuoJi are setting up personal account books for everyday expense tracking. In this part of the product, they are trying to manage account-book-specific settings, including category structure such as food breakfast and food dinner, while also allowing for other non-transaction settings.

### Brand Personality

The interface should feel concise, clear, and intuitive. It should feel warm and everyday rather than technical, futuristic, or system-heavy.

### Aesthetic Direction

The preferred direction is warm daily-life utility: calm, approachable, and low-friction. It should avoid overt tech aesthetics, avoid looking like a dashboard for engineers, and keep the experience grounded in practical household money management.

### Design Principles

- Put the user's next action in plain sight without making the page feel busy.
- Group account-book settings by mental model, not by implementation convenience.
- Favor warm, human utility over abstract product polish or tech-brand styling.
- Use supporting copy only when it reduces uncertainty; do not restate what the UI already says.
- Keep category management prominent as a likely next step, while placing destructive actions at the bottom as rare, subordinate actions.
